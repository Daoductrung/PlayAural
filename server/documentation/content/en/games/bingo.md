\*\*Bingo\*\*

\*\*What is Bingo?\*\*

Bingo is a friendly number game for 2 to 12 players. Everyone listens to the same caller, but each player has a different card. As numbers are called, you mark the matching squares on your card and listen for the pattern chosen by the host.

There are no turns. All players follow the calls and work on their cards at the same time.

\*\*How to win\*\*

Complete the chosen pattern using numbers that have actually been called, then choose \*\*Call Bingo!\*\* or press B. The game checks your card before awarding the win.

Simply completing a pattern is not enough: you must call Bingo while the round is still running. The first valid call wins.

\*\*Your Bingo card\*\*

Your card has five rows and five columns headed B-I-N-G-O. Each column uses its traditional range:

\* \*\*B:\*\* 1 through 15.
\* \*\*I:\*\* 16 through 30.
\* \*\*N:\*\* 31 through 45.
\* \*\*G:\*\* 46 through 60.
\* \*\*O:\*\* 61 through 75.

The center is a free square. It begins marked and counts toward any pattern that crosses it.

When you move around the card, each square announces its letter, number, and whether it is marked. For example, you might hear “B 7, not marked” or “G 52, marked.”

\*\*Playing a round\*\*

After a short opening pause, you hear the ball cage and then the next number. If that number appears on your card, mark it. Press Enter again if you need to remove a mark. The marking sound and confirmation are private, so other players do not hear you working on your card.

The game lets you mark any square. This makes it easy to correct your card, but it also means you are responsible for following the caller. If a completed pattern contains a number that has not been called, your Bingo call is rejected and the game tells you which number caused the problem.

You may keep marking while waiting for the next number. Marking and Bingo calls pause briefly while another card is being checked.

\*\*Calling Bingo\*\*

Call Bingo as soon as your marked squares complete the selected pattern. A short drum roll plays while the card is checked.

If the card is valid, the round ends and the winning numbers are announced. For Blackout, the game skips the long list and simply announces the winner.

If the card is not ready, play continues. You cannot immediately submit the same unchanged card again; change a mark or wait for another number first. This prevents repeated calls from holding up the table.

\*\*Winning patterns\*\*

The host chooses one pattern before the round begins:

\* \*\*Any Line:\*\* Mark all five squares in one horizontal row, vertical column, or diagonal.
\* \*\*Four Corners:\*\* Mark the four corner squares.
\* \*\*Letter X:\*\* Mark both diagonals.
\* \*\*Blackout:\*\* Mark the entire card.

The free center counts automatically whenever it belongs to the pattern.

\*\*Playing on desktop\*\*

The main game view contains only your 5-by-5 card, keeping arrow-key navigation predictable for screen readers.

Use the arrow keys to move and Enter to mark or unmark a square. Press B to call Bingo, R to repeat the last number, or C to open the called-number list. These commands are also available from the in-game actions menu opened with Escape; they are not mixed into the card itself.

\*\*Playing on Web and mobile\*\*

On touch devices, the card is followed by buttons for \*\*Call Bingo!\*\*, \*\*Repeat the last number\*\*, and \*\*View called numbers\*\*. This keeps the actions easy to reach without breaking the card’s 5-by-5 layout.

You can also call Bingo from the card with a deliberate hold gesture. On Web, press and hold a square. With mobile self-voicing, double-tap and hold with one finger. Other mobile accessibility modes can use their usual long-press or long-click action. The visible \*\*Call Bingo!\*\* button is always available as an alternative.

\*\*Options\*\*

\* \*\*Winning pattern:\*\* Any Line, Four Corners, Letter X, or Blackout. The default is Any Line.
\* \*\*Calling pace:\*\* A target of 5, 15, 30, 45, or 60 seconds between announced numbers. The default is 15 seconds.

After every announcement, the game leaves at least four seconds for players to call Bingo. Because the ball-cage sound also needs time to finish, the fastest setting may run a little slower than its five-second target. The final number receives the full selected interval.

\*\*Information actions\*\*

\* \*\*Repeat the last number:\*\* Hear the most recently announced letter and number again.
\* \*\*View called numbers:\*\* Open a live list with the total called so far and the ten most recent calls.
\* \*\*Round status:\*\* Hear how long remains before the next number, whether a number is being drawn, or whose card is being checked. Bingo has no player turns, so this replaces the usual “whose turn” response.

Spectators can use the public information actions but cannot see a card, mark squares, or call Bingo.

\*\*End of the round\*\*

The first valid Bingo call ends the round. If all 75 numbers are announced and nobody calls a valid Bingo before the final window closes, the round ends without a winner.

\*\*Keyboard shortcuts\*\*

\* \*\*Left and Right Arrows:\*\* Move between the B-I-N-G-O columns.
\* \*\*Up and Down Arrows:\*\* Move within the current column.
\* \*\*Enter:\*\* Mark or unmark the selected square.
\* \*\*B:\*\* Call Bingo.
\* \*\*R:\*\* Repeat the last number.
\* \*\*C:\*\* View called numbers.
\* \*\*T:\*\* Hear the current round status.
\* \*\*Escape:\*\* Open the in-game actions menu.
