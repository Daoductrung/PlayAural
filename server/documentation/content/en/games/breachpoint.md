\*\*Breach Point\*\*

\*\*What is Breach Point?\*\*

Breach Point is PlayAural's turn-based take on Counter-Strike, the famous series of tactical action video games. You do not need to have played Counter-Strike—or any shooter—to enjoy it. This guide introduces every term and rule you need.

Each combat round is a tense mission between two equal squads. One squad attacks as T, short for Terrorists. T carries the bomb, chooses between two bombsites, and tries to plant and protect it. The other squad defends as CT, short for Counter-Terrorists. CT guards the approaches, stops the plant, or defuses the bomb before it explodes. These names describe the two roles in the match.

Your first battlefield is Dust, a sun-baked city district of open streets, cramped tunnels, high walkways, and two bombsites called A and B. Every route offers a different risk. Long streets reward rifles and sniper fire; tight corners reward quick weapons, grenades, and teamwork.

The action unfolds one turn at a time, so victory comes from choices rather than reflexes. A legal shot has a predictable result. What matters is where you stand, when you move, what you buy, and whether your squad can read the other side before time runs out.

The squads swap roles halfway through the match, but your teammates and score stay together until the end.

\*\*How to win\*\*

Every combat round is worth one point.

\* T wins by eliminating every CT player before a defuse, or by protecting a planted bomb until it explodes.
\* CT wins by eliminating every T player before the bomb is planted, preventing a plant for six tactical rounds, or defusing a planted bomb.

Once the bomb is planted, eliminating every T player does not end the round. CT must still defuse before the bomb explodes.

The first squad to reach the selected match target wins. MR12—the default—is first to 13.

\*\*Glossary\*\*

\* \*\*T:\*\* The Terrorist side. T carries the bomb and attacks either bombsite.
\* \*\*CT:\*\* The Counter-Terrorist side. CT defends the sites and can defuse a planted bomb.
\* \*\*Combat round:\*\* One fight for one point. Eliminated players return in the next combat round.
\* \*\*Tactical round:\*\* One pass through the living players. The pre-plant clock advances after everyone has had a chance to act.
\* \*\*Turn or activation:\*\* Your chance to spend action points. A normal turn begins with 2 AP.
\* \*\*AP:\*\* Action points, spent on movement, attacks, utility, weapons, and the bomb.
\* \*\*Area:\*\* A named part of Dust. Areas are used for menus, movement, and callouts.
\* \*\*Mid:\*\* The central route. From Mid, players can pressure Short A, cross Mid Doors toward CT, or enter Lower Tunnels toward B.
\* \*\*Bombsite A and Bombsite B:\*\* The two areas where T may plant. Players often shorten these names to A and B.
\* \*\*Long A:\*\* The open route through Long Doors toward A. It rewards range and early control.
\* \*\*Short A:\*\* The tighter raised route from Catwalk into A.
\* \*\*Tunnels:\*\* The enclosed route from Outside Tunnels through Upper Tunnels toward B, with a Lower Tunnels branch into Mid.
\* \*\*Line of sight:\*\* A clear firing lane between two areas. A movement connection is not automatically a firing lane.
\* \*\*Fog of war:\*\* Enemy positions are hidden until your squad has line of sight to them.
\* \*\*Hold angle:\*\* Prepare a weapon on one area so it may react when an enemy enters.
\* \*\*Response:\*\* A short interrupt that lets one opponent contest a held-angle entry, plant, or defuse.
\* \*\*Evasion:\*\* Protection earned by ending a turn with unused AP under the conditions explained below.

\*\*Players and teams\*\*

The game supports 2v2, 3v3, 4v4, and 5v5: 4, 6, 8, or 10 active players split into equal squads. The host may arrange the squads before the match. Spectators can follow public information but cannot act or inspect a team's private status.

\*\*The start of a combat round\*\*

Living players spawn with 100 health and their side's free pistol: Glock for T, USP-S for CT. The bomb is assigned randomly to one living T player. Each player then receives a private buy turn.

The buy menu opens on a read-only loadout summary. Below it are teammate information, item categories, ground weapons, refunds, and buying for a teammate. Purchases are grouped into Equipment, Pistols, Mid-Tier, Rifles, and Grenades. Choose \*\*Finish buying\* when ready. After everyone finishes, three beeps lead into combat.

\*\*Your turn\*\*

The first combat row summarizes your health, equipped weapon, and anything you can currently see. The action categories follow:

\* \*\*Move:\*\* Hear a description of your area, then choose a connected destination.
\* \*\*Attack:\*\* Fire at an enemy you can personally see and reach with the equipped weapon.
\* \*\*Use utility:\*\* Choose a grenade, then its target area. Your own area is marked clearly.
\* \*\*Hold angle:\*\* Prepare a firing lane for a later reaction.
\* \*\*Objective:\*\* Plant, defuse, or recover the dropped bomb when legal.
\* \*\*Weapons and reload:\*\* Switch weapons or reload.
\* \*\*Ground weapons:\*\* Recover a weapon in your current area.

Spend up to 2 AP in any legal order. Using the last AP ends the turn automatically; otherwise choose \*\*End activation\* or press E. After a choice, you return to the category that opened it. While footsteps, a burst, a grenade, or a response is resolving, the combat menu stays visible but locked and explains what is still happening.

\*\*Movement, contact, and fog of war\*\*

Moving to a connected area costs 1 AP. Allies and enemies may share an area, so entering an occupied area is legal and creates point-blank danger. Leaving an area shared with a living enemy is a \*\*Disengage\* and costs 2 AP.

You always know your teammates' locations and public combat state. A living enemy appears only while at least one living teammate can see that enemy's area. Team sightings are shared, but you may shoot only through your own line of sight. Smoke blocks sight into and out of its area; players sharing the smoked area still see one another.

When a contact disappears from every team sightline, it becomes hidden again. Hidden movement never reveals the destination. Eliminations are different: the kill feed is global and always names the shooter, victim, weapon, and area, so an eliminated player's final location remains public.

Before a plant, CT learns the bomb carrier only by seeing that player. CT may see and guard a dropped bomb but cannot pick it up. T always knows its own bomb state. A planted bomb and its site are public.

\*\*Gunfights, armor, and evasion\*\*

A shot normally costs 1 AP. The enemy must be alive, inside the weapon's range, and in your personal line of sight. Friendly fire with guns is disabled. Damage is greatest when both players share an area and falls with distance.

Kevlar starts at 100 armor and absorbs a weapon-specific share of damage before health is lost. Reaching 0 health eliminates that player for the rest of the combat round.

If you end a turn without firing or holding an angle, unused AP becomes up to 2 evasion against the next attack. Evasion can completely avoid a single pistol round, but rifle and SMG bursts still land at least one round. AWP fire cannot be dodged; each evasion point reduces its damage by 10 instead. Evasion protects against only one attack and expires at your next turn.

Camping weakens evasion. Preparing it repeatedly in the same area loses 1 point each time until none remains. Moving to another area resets this decay.

\*\*Weapons\*\*

Switching between your primary and pistol is free. Each weapon keeps its own per-turn attack limit, so switching does not restore attacks already spent with that weapon. A later shot in the same turn may be weaker from recoil. Every attack consumes ammunition; reloading costs 1 AP and breaks a held angle.

\* \*\*Glock:\*\* Free T pistol. Range 1, two attacks per turn; 38 damage at range 0 and 30 at range 1.
\* \*\*USP-S:\*\* Free CT pistol. Range 1, two attacks per turn; 36 damage at range 0 and 32 at range 1.
\* \*\*Desert Eagle — $700:\*\* Range 2, one precise shot; 72, 58, or 44 damage.
\* \*\*MAC-10 — $1,050, T only:\*\* Range 1, two fast bursts; 76 or 36 damage, with a 55% follow-up. Best up close against light buys.
\* \*\*MP9 — $1,250, CT only:\*\* Range 1, two controlled bursts; 68 or 48 damage, with a 60% follow-up.
\* \*\*Nova — $1,050:\*\* Range 1, one shell; 96 damage point-blank but only 38 at range 1. Reloads up to two shells without discarding those already loaded.
\* \*\*Galil AR — $1,800, T only:\*\* Range 2, two bursts that must choose different targets; 74, 56, or 34 damage, with a 55% follow-up.
\* \*\*FAMAS — $1,950, CT only:\*\* Range 2, two bursts that may share a target; 64, 50, or 32 damage, with a 60% follow-up.
\* \*\*SSG 08 — $1,700:\*\* Range 3, one shot for 88, 84, 80, or 74 damage. A normal shot requires a held angle on the target's exact area.
\* \*\*AK-47 — $2,700, T only:\*\* Range 2, one heavy burst for 92, 69, or 46 damage.
\* \*\*M4 — $2,900, CT only:\*\* Range 2, two bursts for 72, 54, or 36 damage, with a 65% follow-up.
\* \*\*AWP — $4,750:\*\* Range 3. A normal shot costs 2 AP and requires a held angle on the target's exact area. It deals 125, 120, 115, or 110 damage. A held-angle response fires at full strength.

Holding an angle costs 1 AP and ends the turn. When an enemy enters the watched area through a clear sightline, movement pauses and the closest eligible watcher may fire or pass. Moving, switching, reloading, using utility, handling the bomb, taking damage, becoming flashed, or firing breaks the angle. Passing keeps it.

\*\*Utility and equipment\*\*

You may carry four utility items in total and may throw into your own area or a connected area.

\* \*\*Kevlar — $650:\*\* Gives 100 armor.
\* \*\*Defuse Kit — $400, CT only:\*\* Reduces defusing from 2 AP to 1.
\* \*\*Smoke Grenade — $300, carry 1:\*\* Lasts through the current and next tactical round. It blocks sight and extinguishes fire.
\* \*\*Flashbang — $200, carry 2:\*\* Everyone in the target area except the thrower loses 1 AP on their next turn and loses any held angle. Teammates can be flashed.
\* \*\*HE Grenade — $300, carry 1:\*\* Deals up to 45 damage in the target area. Armor and evasion reduce it. Damage to the thrower or an ally is halved.
\* \*\*Molotov — $400, T only, carry 1:\*\* Ignites for 20 damage and leaves fire through the next tactical round.
\* \*\*Incendiary Grenade — $500, CT only, carry 1:\*\* Uses the same fire rules as the Molotov.

Your team hears your utility callout. The enemy receives the exact callout only when it can see the throw. Smoke prevents fire from igniting and extinguishes fire already burning in the same area.

\*\*Planting and defusing\*\*

Only the T bomb carrier may plant, and only at A or B. Starting the plant costs 1 AP and ends that player's turn, but the bomb is not planted yet. One living CT receives a response. Any shot that damages health or armor interrupts the plant; otherwise it completes after the response. The normal turn order then resumes from where it was interrupted.

CT cannot start a defuse while planting is still in progress. Once planted, the bomb receives its full three-tactical-round fuse even if T planted on the final pre-plant turn.

A CT at the bomb may start defusing for 2 AP, or 1 AP with a Defuse Kit. The action ends that CT's turn and gives one living T a response. Any health or armor damage interrupts the defuse; a fully evaded attack does not. If the response ends without damage, the defuse succeeds.

When the final full fuse round ends, the bomb enters its final arming sequence. It is then too late to defuse.

\*\*Dropped weapons\*\*

An eliminated player's primary falls with its remaining ammunition. If there is no primary, a purchased pistol falls instead; free starting pistols do not. Either side may spend 1 AP to pick up a gun in its area. The old gun in that slot drops, the recovered gun becomes equipped, and any held angle breaks.

If the final elimination wins the round and the survivor still has enough AP beside the dropped weapon, the round pauses once so that survivor may pick it up or skip. All ground weapons disappear when the round ends.

\*\*Economy, refunds, and teammate buys\*\*

Each regulation half starts at $800. Survivors keep weapons, remaining armor, equipment, utility, and cash. Eliminated players keep cash but lose purchased gear and receive the free pistol for their new round. Cash is capped at $16,000. Halftime resets everyone to $800; each overtime half starts at $10,000.

A purchase may be refunded for its full price until you finish that same buy turn, provided the item is still available. A newly bought gun remains refundable after another purchase drops it. Gear carried from an older round and battlefield pickups cannot be refunded.

You may buy a gun for a living teammate, including one whose buy turn has already passed. The recipient may accept or decline. Accepting equips and commits the gift. Declining drops it at the buyer's position, where the buyer may refund it or leave it on the ground.

Round winners receive $3,250 for elimination or time, and $3,500 for defuse or detonation. Consecutive loss income rises from $1,400 to $3,400. A successful plant or defuse pays that player $300; a T loss after planting also pays each T player a $600 plant bonus.

\*\*The Dust routes\*\*

\* \*\*Long A:\*\* T Spawn → Outside Long → Long Doors → Long A → A Ramp → Bombsite A. Pit is a protected pocket beside Long.
\* \*\*Short A:\*\* T Spawn → Mid → Catwalk → Short A → Bombsite A.
\* \*\*Mid:\*\* T Spawn → Mid → Mid Doors → CT Mid → CT Spawn. Lower Tunnels also joins Mid around a blind corner.
\* \*\*B:\*\* T Spawn → Outside Tunnels → Upper Tunnels → B Tunnels → Bombsite B. CT normally enters through B Doors.

Movement and sight are separate. Mid and Lower Tunnels connect around a blind corner, while long firing lanes link Pit to A, T Spawn to CT Mid, Mid to CT Spawn, and CT Spawn to B Tunnels. These lanes are where SSG 08 and AWP earn their range.

\*\*Match formats and options\*\*

\* \*\*MR7:\*\* Seven regulation rounds per half; first to 8.
\* \*\*MR12:\*\* Twelve rounds per half; first to 13. Default.
\* \*\*MR15:\*\* Fifteen rounds per half; first to 16.

A tied regulation match either ends in a draw or enters repeatable MR3 overtime, according to the host's \*\*Tied match resolution\* option. Each MR3 period has three rounds per side; 4 wins takes the period, while 3–3 starts another. The six-round pre-plant clock and three-round bomb fuse are fixed.

\*\*Information actions\*\*

\* \*\*Read health and armor:\*\* Your current health and armor.
\* \*\*Read your loadout and tactical status:\*\* Your area, side, weapons, ammunition, local ground weapons and effects, equipment, utility, cash, AP, evasion, and held angle.
\* \*\*Read tactical map:\*\* A live map of routes, firing lanes, known effects, visible ground weapons, teammates, and currently known enemies.
\* \*\*Read teammate status:\*\* A live team-only roster with public teammate location, health, armor, equipped weapon, money, and state.
\* \*\*Read known enemy status:\*\* A live roster containing only enemies whose location is currently known, plus eliminated enemies made public by the kill feed.
\* \*\*Read bomb status:\*\* The bomb information currently known to your side.
\* \*\*Check scores:\*\* One short line with the current round and T/CT score. Detailed scores adds squad, side, target, and overtime information.

Desktop players find information actions in the actions menu and can use the shortcuts below. Touch players receive the same actions as ordered buttons.

\*\*Keyboard shortcuts\*\*

\* \*\*H:\*\* Read health and armor.
\* \*\*P:\*\* Read your loadout and tactical status.
\* \*\*M:\*\* Open the tactical map.
\* \*\*V:\*\* Open teammate status.
\* \*\*W:\*\* Open known enemy status.
\* \*\*O:\*\* Read bomb status.
\* \*\*S:\*\* Read the brief score.
\* \*\*Shift+S:\*\* Open detailed scores.
\* \*\*E:\*\* Finish buying or end your combat turn, according to the current phase.
\* \*\*1 through 9:\*\* Open or select the numbered buy-menu item.
\* \*\*X:\*\* Return from the current buy or combat submenu.

\* \*\*T:\*\* Check whose turn it is.
