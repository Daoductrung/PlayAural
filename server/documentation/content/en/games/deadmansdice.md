\*\*Dead Man's Dice\*\*

Dead Man's Dice is a game of hidden rolls, rising bids, and nerve. You know the five dice beneath your own cup, but nobody can see the whole table. Every bid might be an honest estimate, a careful bluff, or one lie too many.

There are no points. Lose a challenge and you drink poison. The second dose eliminates you. The last survivor wins.

\*\*Taking A Seat\*\*

The game supports 2 to 4 players. Everyone begins with five six-sided dice and no poison.

When the match starts, the tavern soundscape fades in during a short five-second introduction. The game announces how many players are at the table, which rules are active, and how ones are counted. The first round begins only after that introduction finishes.

At the start of every round, each survivor rolls five dice beneath a cup. Your roll is spoken privately to you. Other players know how many dice are in play, but not what you rolled.

\*\*What A Bid Means\*\*

A bid names a quantity and a face. If you bid “four fives,” you are claiming that at least four dice across the entire table count as fives. You are allowed to bid more than your own cup contains because the claim covers everyone's hidden dice.

Your bid does not have to be true. Bluffing is part of the game.

\*\*How To Raise\*\*

Every new bid must move forward on the bidding ladder. You can:

\* Keep the same quantity and choose a higher face.
\* Increase the quantity and choose any face.

After a bid of four fives, you may bid four sixes, five ones, five twos, or anything higher. You may not bid four fours or three sixes because both move backward.

PlayAural asks you to choose a face first. It then shows only the quantities that are legal for that face. This keeps the menu short while leaving the decision entirely to you.

\*\*Traditional Rules\*\*

Traditional is the default ruleset. Ones are wild when a bid names twos, threes, fours, fives, or sixes. When the table checks a bid of four fives, every five and every one counts toward the total.

A bid on ones is the exception. Only actual ones count when the named face is one.

\*\*Basic Rules\*\*

Basic rules count every face literally. A five counts as a five, a one counts as a one, and ones are never wild. All other rules remain the same.

\*\*Your Turn\*\*

If nobody has bid yet, you must make the opening bid. After a bid exists, you have three choices:

\* Raise the bid.
\* Call liar if you believe the bid is too high.
\* Call Spot On if you believe the bid is exactly right.

You cannot pass. Once you act, play moves to the next survivor.

\*\*Calling Liar\*\*

Calling liar ends the bidding for that round. Every cup is lifted, one at a time, and the matching dice are counted.

\* If the actual count is lower than the bid, the bidder loses.
\* If the actual count meets or exceeds the bid, the challenger loses.

The loser drinks one dose of poison. If they survive, they open the next round. If that dose eliminates them, the next living player in the established order opens instead.

\*\*Calling Spot On\*\*

Spot On is available under both rulesets. It is a higher-risk call: you are claiming that the bid matches the table exactly.

\* If the count is exact, every other survivor drinks one dose of poison.
\* If the count is not exact, only the Spot On caller drinks.

The caller opens the next round if still alive. If the caller is eliminated, the opening turn passes to the next survivor. A correct Spot On can eliminate several players at once and may end the match immediately.

\*\*Poison And Elimination\*\*

Your first dose leaves you alive but in danger. Your second dose eliminates you. Eliminated players keep hearing the public game state but no longer roll, bid, or challenge.

The match ends as soon as only one survivor remains.

\*\*Keeping Track Of The Table\*\*

The game never asks you to remember information that is still publicly available:

\* \*\*Read your dice:\*\* Hear your current hidden roll again.
\* \*\*Read table:\*\* Review the rules, dice in play, current bid, active turn, and every player's poison.
\* \*\*Review bids:\*\* Hear every bid from the current round in order.
\* \*\*Review last reveal:\*\* Review each opened cup, the final count, and who lost the challenge.
\* \*\*Whose turn and who is at the table:\*\* Reorient yourself without changing the game.

These information views report facts; they do not estimate odds or recommend a move.

\*\*Spatial Audio\*\*

With headphones, every seat has a fixed place around the table. Dice, bids, lifted cups, poison, and deaths come from the player who caused them. The crowd, bar, glasses, fan, and occasional pouring remain anchored around the room. Turning the listening position from one seat to another changes the direction naturally. Spectators listen from the centre of the table.

Speech always carries the complete game state. Spatial audio adds presence and orientation without hiding information from players who cannot use it.

\*\*Table Option\*\*

The host can choose one ruleset before the match:

\* \*\*Traditional, default:\*\* Ones are wild for bids on twos through sixes.
\* \*\*Basic:\*\* Every face counts only itself.

Both rulesets use five dice per survivor, allow liar and Spot On calls, eliminate on the second poison dose, and end with one survivor.

\*\*Keyboard Shortcuts\*\*

\* \*\*1 through 6:\*\* Start a bid on that face.
\* \*\*C:\*\* Call liar.
\* \*\*O:\*\* Call Spot On.
\* \*\*D:\*\* Read your dice.
\* \*\*V:\*\* Read the table.
\* \*\*B:\*\* Review this round's bids.
\* \*\*R:\*\* Review the last reveal.
\* \*\*T:\*\* Check whose turn it is.
\* \*\*Ctrl+U:\*\* Hear who is at the table.
