\*\*Last Outpost\*\*

\*\*The last light\*\*

The generator stopped before sunset. Since then, the radio has offered nothing but static. Beyond the barricades, something drags its feet through the dark. Then another set of footsteps joins it.

You have reached the last outpost still worth defending. There are supplies here, a radio that might work again, and enough walls to buy a little time. Alone or with companions, you need a way out before the barricades give.

Last Outpost is a cooperative survival game for one to four players. Its inspirations include Call of Duty Zombies, Zombicide, Tiny Epic Zombies, and Run, Fight, or Die. It has its own turn-based rules: you choose actions from menus, hear them unfold, and plan your next move. You do not need shooter experience or fast reflexes. Take time to listen and think; reading menus and talking to your team never advances the horde.

\*\*How to win\*\*

Survive every wave and meet your mode's objectives. In a group, everyone wins or loses together. Killing the last zombie is not enough if a mission remains unfinished. In Operations and Classic Extraction, finish the power and radio repairs before clearing the final wave or the run ends in defeat.

Open Objectives with Q to check the requirements. It separates mandatory work from optional improvements and shows your progress, current mission, and remaining time. C opens Teammates, a separate list of squad positions and health.

Every mode ends in defeat if the entire squad goes down, apart from the one emergency recovery available in a true solo run. A wave also has a limit of 12 horde phases. You cannot hold out forever.

\*\*A few useful terms\*\*

\* HP, or health: how much damage you can take. At zero, you are downed and need help.
\* AP, or action points: what you spend during your turn. A normal turn gives you 3 AP.
\* Zone: a named place on the map, such as the Workshop. Moving follows the connections between zones.
\* Cycle: one turn for each upright survivor, followed by a horde phase.
\* Horde phase: the enemies' chance to break barricades, move, and attack. Mission deadlines count these phases, not seconds.
\* Wave: a whole attack, including enemies arriving later and any assigned mission. It can last several cycles.
\* Preparation: the shopping break between waves. Everyone marks Ready to start the next wave.
\* Credits: your own money for equipment. Parts are a separate, shared supply used for gates, repairs, and infrastructure.
\* Pack: enemies of the same type in the same zone, on the same side of a barricade. Attack menus group them together.
\* Inside and outside: whether enemies have crossed an entrance barricade. An enemy can be inside your perimeter even in an outdoor zone.

\*\*Your first match\*\*

For a first attempt, choose Abandoned Outpost and Recruit. You can bring friends, add bots, or go alone. Bots use the same supplies and rules as human survivors; they are teammates, not extra lives.

You start in combat with 6 health, an M1911 pistol holding 7 rounds, 14 reserve rounds, and 400 credits. The first preparation break comes after wave one. Until then, make those pistol rounds count.

When your turn begins, try this:

\* Check Health, then open the Enemy inspector to find the nearest threats.
\* Read the next horde forecast. It predicts what happens if the squad takes no further action.
\* Decide what matters most: preventing a bite, moving toward a task, or helping someone else.
\* Spend your AP. For example, move once and shoot twice, if the route and target allow it.
\* End your turn when you are finished. Unspent AP does not carry over.

Two pistol hits kill a full-health walker. A wounded walker with 1 health left can be finished with a knife if it is inside your zone. That saves ammunition, but stepping into a crowd just to save one round can cost you far more.

\*\*Finding your actions\*\*

The main menu starts with your health, AP, and equipped weapon. Below it are these categories:

\* Move: choose an open route. The menu describes your current zone; each destination previews its AP cost, enemies, teammates, and barricade.
\* Attack: shoot, use your knife, or throw a grenade at a valid target.
\* Weapons and reload: switch weapons or reload. Its main-menu label shows your loaded and reserve ammunition.
\* Aid and cargo: heal, revive, hand over cargo, or put it down.
\* Objectives and surroundings: work on equipment, operate a mission terminal, open a gate, or repair a barricade.
\* Supplies and upgrades: buy equipment and ammunition.

Available beside a category means it contains an action you can perform now. Brace and End turn sit below the categories during combat. During preparation, Ready for next wave takes their place.

After a combat action, you return to its category button on the main menu, ready to choose again. Purchases keep the shop open so you can buy several things. X or Back to main menu returns without acting. Between your turns, the main menu stays visible and you can still check information.

On a touchscreen, information buttons sit below your actions: health, enemies, forecast, teammates, objectives, inventory, then map. With a keyboard, use the shortcuts at the end of this guide. F1 describes the selected item; Ctrl+F1 opens this manual.

\*\*Turns, movement, and cover\*\*

Most actions cost 1 AP: shooting, knife attacks, reloading, healing, repairing, and working on objectives. Switching between your pistol and primary weapon is free. Costs appear on the action before you choose it.

Moving along one open connection costs 1 AP. Leaving a zone with enemies inside costs 2 AP. Carrying cargo adds another AP to either cost. Outside enemies behind a barricade do not impose the escape surcharge.

After every standing survivor finishes, the horde acts. The next cycle rotates who goes first and restores AP. A revived survivor gets their next turn in the following cycle.

Brace costs 1 AP. You take the next attack in your zone ahead of unbraced teammates and reduce its damage by one, possibly to zero. The protection is used by that attack and does not stack; unused protection expires next cycle. Bracing can protect a wounded ally, but it does not pull enemies from distant zones.

You can walk and check information while reloading. Wait for the reload to finish before shooting, changing weapons, doing other tasks or ending your turn.

\*\*The horde\*\*

\* Walker: 3 health, 1 damage, moves one connection.
\* Hound: 2 health, 1 damage, can move two connections. Appears from wave three.
\* Brute: 8 health, 2 damage, moves one connection. Appears from wave four.

Enemies outside an entrance attack its barricade. Breaking it and entering are separate steps; listen for the breach and use the forecast before deciding whether to stay. Inside enemies follow open routes toward survivors or threatened mission sites. A walker or brute gets one step per horde phase; a hound gets two. Breaking a barricade, entering, moving one connection, or biting each uses a step. A hound can therefore move and then bite in one phase; a walker that has just moved must wait. Each enemy bites at most once per phase. When survivors are equally close along open routes, gunfire can draw enemies toward the noisier zone. Noise does not pull them past a closer survivor, and it clears after the horde phase.

Not every enemy in a crowded zone can bite at once. Each open approach provides two contact slots, with at least two slots in any zone. A brute uses two slots; a walker or hound uses one. Enemies waiting behind the attackers still block work and mission interactions.

A wave arrives in groups. The opening group is followed by reserves, normally after horde phases two and four. Newly arriving enemies act no earlier than the following phase. The wave announcement and Map give exact numbers and arrival times.

From wave two, Standard and Veteran can bring an early rush, fog, heavier barricade damage, or expensive ammunition. Fog limits guns to one connection; an early rush brings the reserve groups after phases one and three. Check the announced condition rather than assuming the last wave's plan still works.

\*\*Weapons and supplies\*\*

Gunfire costs 1 AP and has fixed damage. The most wounded enemy in the selected pack is hit first. A closed gate blocks the route; an intact entrance barricade protects outside enemies from your attacks. Long shots need an open, straight firing lane.

\* M1911 pistol: 2 damage, range zero to one connection, magazine 7, one round per shot.
\* AK rifle: 3 damage, range zero to one connection, magazine 30, three rounds per burst. Upgraded damage: 4.
\* Shotgun: 4 damage to each of up to two enemies in one pack, same zone only, magazine 6, one shell per shot. Upgraded damage: 6 each.
\* Marksman rifle: 6 damage, range one to two connections, magazine 3, one round per shot. It cannot fire with enemies inside your zone. Upgraded damage: 9.

You carry a pistol and one primary weapon. Each primary costs 400 credits. The first purchase of that weapon gives a full magazine and two reserve magazines. If you replace it and later buy it back, it has the ammunition you left in it. Replacing a primary gives no refund; its upgrade is still there if you buy that type again.

Reloading fills the equipped gun from its reserve. A reserve pack holds up to one magazine and normally costs 60 credits, or 90 during a shortage. You can hold three reserve magazines per weapon. A partial pack costs proportionally less, rounded up. During combat, buy ammunition for 1 AP at the map's ammo station while its zone is clear. During preparation, shop from any zone without spending AP.

A knife deals 1 damage inside your zone for 1 AP. A grenade costs 150 credits; carry one. Throw it for 1 AP into your zone or a visible adjacent zone to deal 3 damage to up to four enemies inside. Grenades do not harm teammates.

A medical kit costs 100 credits; carry two. Spend a kit and 1 AP to restore up to 3 health to yourself or an upright teammate in your zone. A kit cannot revive a downed survivor.

Once power is restored, you can buy a 500-credit upgrade for your primary and up to two perks at 300 credits each:

\* Endurance: gain 2 maximum and current health once.
\* Field Medic: revive for 1 AP instead of 2.
\* Quick Hands: your first reload each wave costs no AP. It still needs reserve ammunition and time to finish.

\*\*Doors, barricades, and infrastructure\*\*

Opening a gate costs 1 AP and shared parts. It stays open for everyone, including the horde. A shortcut may help a rescue while also giving enemies a new approach. The gate description lists the connections it opens; check them before spending parts.

Operations and Classic Extraction require power and radio restoration. On every map, open the service gate for 2 parts, repair the power supply in two actions using 2 more parts, open radio access for 3 parts, then repair the radio in three actions using 3 more parts. That is 10 shared parts before optional gates or repairs. Each repair action costs 1 AP; the parts are spent when you first work on that device. You can leave and return without losing progress.

Work, gate opening, and repairs require a zone clear of enemies inside. Repairing an entrance costs 1 AP and 1 part, restores two barricade strength up to four, and is limited to once per entrance per cycle. Keep enough parts for mandatory work before rebuilding every barrier.

\*\*Mission tasks\*\*

Objectives shows the exact places, progress, and deadline. A clear zone means no enemies inside; enemies still outside its barricade do not block the task.

\* Cargo recovery: reach the pickup, take the crate for 1 AP, carry it to the destination, then deliver for 1 AP. Pickup and delivery zones must be clear. While carrying, only your pistol can fire and movement costs one extra AP. Hand it to an upright teammate in the same clear zone for 1 AP, or drop it for 1 AP even when surrounded. If you go down, the crate falls where you stood.
\* Coordinated relay: two different survivors activate two clear terminal zones in the same cycle, for 1 AP each. Both must remain upright at their own terminals through the horde phase, with both sites clear afterward. Otherwise the attempt resets. In true solo, terminals stay activated across cycles so you can visit both. A downed teammate still counts toward a multiplayer team.
\* Sector defense: keep an upright survivor in the marked zone and clear it of inside enemies at the end of two horde phases. An empty or contested phase removes one progress. There is no extra button to press: holding the ground is the task.

Each completed mission awards 40 credits to every survivor and one shared part. An active mission draws extra walkers every second horde phase: one for teams of up to three, two for a four-person team. Kills earn no money. Delaying a task drains ammunition rather than creating an endless reward source.

\*\*Modes\*\*

\* Operations: six waves, three required missions on waves two, four, and six, plus power and radio restoration. The missions are cargo recovery, coordinated relay, and sector defense in a shuffled order. Failing any one loses the operation.
\* Siege: six waves defending a relay with 12 integrity. Each wave marks a defense site. Enemies inside that site remove one integrity each per horde phase, up to two. Missing a mission deadline removes four. Continue protecting the site until the wave ends, even after completing its task. Integrity must stay above zero. Power and radio are optional; power still unlocks upgrades.
\* Salvage: six waves, each with a cargo mission. Deliver at least four crates. You may abandon a costly delivery, but defeat follows once too few opportunities remain to reach four. Power and radio are optional.
\* Classic Extraction: eight waves, power and radio restoration, and one final delivery. On wave eight, take the extraction supplies from the ammo station to the radio before the deadline. Objectives names this route from the start. Clear it and stock ammunition before collecting the crate.

\*\*Battlefields\*\*

\* Abandoned Outpost: a bare flagpole stands between a rusting workshop and a ward of empty cots. Eight zones surround the cracked Courtyard, with a flooded pump room below the rooftop antenna. Buy ammunition at the Workshop, restore power in the Generator Room, then reach the Radio Roof. The short central routes make this a good first map; opening every shortcut also gives the horde more ways in.
\* Switchyard: empty wagons crowd the rails beneath dead signals and a narrow steel Overpass. Ten zones spread out from the Passenger Depot. Ammunition is in the Maintenance Shed, power in the Switchgear Room, and the transmitter in the Radio Tower. Divide work between the two rail fronts, but keep a route back to anyone who needs help.
\* Floodworks: black water laps against the Landing Quay; beyond it, flooded filters and abandoned laboratories divide the plant into two wings. Across ten zones, your key stops are the Supply Archive for ammunition, Turbine Hall for power and Radio Skybridge for the transmitter. Opening the bulkhead saves a long detour, but lets the horde cross the plant just as easily.

Map shows the routes currently open, gates and enemy entrances. One or two survivors face two entrances; three survivors face three, and four face four. An entrance remains dangerous after its barricade falls. Audio directions stay fixed with north ahead; moving does not turn your view. Check the listed routes before following a nearby sound: a wall or closed gate may stand between you and its source.

\*\*Going down and helping each other\*\*

At zero health, you have two future horde phases to be revived. The phase that knocked you down does not count. A teammate in your zone can revive you for 2 AP, restoring 2 health. Field Medic reduces the cost to 1 AP. After the rescue deadline expires, you wait for the next preparation break.

Revival gives no immediate turn or immunity. Before rescuing into a crowd, consider clearing an attacker, bracing, or saving a kit to heal the survivor afterward. Focus a Revive action and request its description to hear the predicted health after the next horde if nobody else acts.

A run started with one survivor has one emergency recovery. You remain down briefly after the horde finishes, then return with 3 health and 2 AP. Later turns return to 3 AP. Cargo still drops where you fell, and going down a second time ends the run. Losing teammates does not grant an emergency recovery.

\*\*Preparation and difficulty\*\*

Clearing a nonfinal wave gives three shared parts, credits for every survivor, and the recovery allowed by the difficulty. Shopping is free of AP costs. Movement and objective work wait until combat resumes.

In the shop, E or Done shopping and Ready closes it and marks you ready. Outside the shop, E also marks Ready during preparation. X or Back to main menu closes the shop without readying. Pressing E again while already ready leaves you ready; choose Cancel readiness if you need to shop again. The next wave starts when everyone is ready.

During combat, E closes an open shop without ending your turn. In the other action menus, E ends your turn and returns you to the main menu at the category you were using. If you are already on the main menu, the selection stays where it was.

\* Recruit: lowest threat; wave reward 150 plus 25 per wave cleared; surviving players heal 2, downed players return with 3 health; up to 7 free pistol reserve rounds. Mission deadlines are 10 horde phases, without wave conditions.
\* Standard: more enemies; reward 100 plus 15 per wave; survivors heal 1, downed players return with 2; up to 3 free pistol reserve rounds. Deadlines are 9 phases; wave conditions apply.
\* Veteran: greatest pressure; reward 80 plus 15 per wave; no healing for upright survivors, downed players return with 1; no free ammunition. Deadlines are 8 phases; wave conditions apply. Every wasted shot and detour matters.

Healing cannot exceed your maximum HP, and free ammunition cannot overfill your reserve. Harder settings bring more enemies, not tougher individual zombies or weaker guns. The number of survivors at the start determines the horde's size; bots count too. A smaller group faces fewer enemies but still has the same repairs and distances to cover. Going alone is a serious challenge.

\*\*The survivors\*\*

Each survivor speaks as one of these familiar Call of Duty Zombies characters. Their personalities bring flavor to the fight; everyone has the same health, weapon damage and abilities.

\* Dempsey: a hardened soldier who meets danger head-on. His blunt confidence suits the survivor covering the next doorway.
\* Nikolai: a weary veteran with a dark sense of humor. Even when the situation is dreadful, he tends to have a remark ready.
\* Takeo: a disciplined warrior whose resolve comes from duty and honor. His measured manner stands out amid the panic.
\* Richtofen: an unsettling doctor with a fascination for things everyone else would rather escape. His enthusiasm is rarely reassuring.

Character dialogue is in English and can include strong language. The host can turn it off. Tactical announcements still use your chosen language. Listen for nearby teammates, approaching enemies and working machinery to help orient yourself.

\*\*Host options\*\*

\* Map: Abandoned Outpost, Switchyard, or Floodworks. Default: Abandoned Outpost.
\* Mode: Operations, Siege, Salvage, or Classic Extraction. Default: Operations. The mode determines the wave count.
\* Difficulty: Recruit, Standard, or Veteran. Default: Standard.
\* Character dialogue: on or off. Default: on.

One to four active survivors can play, with at least one human. Spectators can inspect public information but cannot act. Survival records reward successful, efficient clears.

\*\*Survival records\*\*

Open Leaderboards from the main menu, choose Last Outpost, then select the mode, difficulty, map and starting lineup. A true solo run has one human and no bots. One human with a bot and two humans are different two-survivor categories.

Only victories count, and fewer horde phases is better. Time spent thinking or reading menus does not affect your record. Everyone credited with the win receives the same result; kills are not scored separately. Tied results share a rank, and your best clear in each category is kept. You can also check My Stats.

A ranked run must keep its original survivors. Removing one or bringing in a different person makes the whole match unranked, so joining near the end cannot earn a free clear. Reconnecting to your own survivor preserves eligibility. A disconnected teammate still counts in the original lineup and can receive the result if their place remains reserved.

With at least two humans, records are split between No bot takeovers and With bot takeovers. The latter applies once a replacement bot performs an action, even if its player returns later. A disconnect by itself does not change the category. Bots added before the match are already part of the starting lineup.

\*\*If someone disconnects\*\*

An unexpected disconnect briefly pauses the match to give the player time to return, then a bot can take over. Leaving deliberately allows an immediate replacement. Bots cannot continue without a connected human survivor: play stays paused until someone returns to their character or the host brings in a human substitute. If the last player deliberately leaves and no host remains to keep the table, the match is cancelled.

\*\*Keyboard reference\*\*

\* H: announce your health and rescue state.
\* W: Enemy inspector, ordered by attack reach and distance.
\* F: next horde forecast, including mission consequences.
\* C: Teammates, or Character in a solo run.
\* Q: Objectives.
\* I: shared parts, your credits, equipment, ammunition, perks and consumables.
\* M: Map, routes, barricades, and incoming enemies.
\* R: reload. 1: equip primary. 2: equip pistol.
\* E: Ready during preparation; close an open combat shop; otherwise end your combat turn.
\* X: return to the main menu without acting. Escape: Actions menu, or close an open information panel.
\* F1: describe the focused item. Ctrl+F1: open this manual.

Close an information panel before using action-navigation shortcuts. Information checks cost no AP and remain available off turn. Take advantage of them: a moment spent checking the route can save an entire wave.
