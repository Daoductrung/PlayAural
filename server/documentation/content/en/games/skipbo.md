\*\*Skip-Bo\*\*

\*\*What is Skip-Bo?\*\*

Skip-Bo is a sequencing card game for 2 to 6 players. Everyone has a personal stock pile, and the goal is to play every card from that pile before anyone else does. Cards are played to four shared building piles in numerical order from 1 through 12.

Your hand helps you reach the cards in your stock pile, but emptying your hand does not win the game. The stock pile is what matters. Skip-Bo wild cards, several personal discard piles, and repeated hand refills give you room to plan long combinations in a single turn.

The current deck contains 168 cards: 12 copies of every number from 1 through 12 and 24 Skip-Bo wild cards.

\*\*How to win\*\*

In an individual game, you win a game as soon as you play the last card from your stock pile.

In a partnership game, both partners must empty their stock piles. Emptying one partner's pile does not end the game; the partnership continues until its other stock pile is empty too.

The default \*\*Single game\* format ends the table game immediately. A \*\*Scored match\* continues across several games until a player or partnership reaches the selected match target.

\*\*Glossary\*\*

\* \*\*Stock pile:\*\* Your personal face-down pile. Its top card is face up and available to play. Empty this pile to win.

\* \*\*Draw pile:\*\* The shared face-down cards used to fill hands.

\* \*\*Hand:\*\* Your private cards. You normally begin each turn with five after drawing.

\* \*\*Building pile:\*\* One of four shared piles in the center. Each builds upward in exact order from 1 to 12.

\* \*\*Discard pile:\*\* One of four personal piles. Any hand card may be placed on one to end a turn, but only its top card can be played later.

\* \*\*Skip-Bo wild:\*\* A wild card that becomes whatever number the chosen building pile needs next.

\* \*\*Game:\*\* One deal, ending when a player or partnership empties the required stock piles.

\* \*\*Scored match:\*\* A series of games whose scores carry forward to a target.

\*\*Players and partnerships\*\*

Individual play supports any table from 2 to 6 players.

Partnerships always use teams of two. Four players form two partnerships; six players form three. The host may review and rearrange the teams before starting. Turn order alternates between partnerships so partners do not take consecutive turns under the normal arrangement.

During your turn, you may play from your own stock and discard piles and from your partner's stock and discard piles. You may not see or play cards from your partner's hand. The physical-game partnership rule also asks partners not to discuss moves while one of them is playing; PlayAural leaves that agreement to the players.

\*\*Setting up a game\*\*

With the \*\*Standard\* stock setting, each player receives 30 stock cards in a 2-to-4-player game and 20 in a 5-or-6-player game. The \*\*Quick 10\* and \*\*Quick 15\* settings instead give every player exactly 10 or 15 stock cards. Only the top card of each stock pile is turned face up.

The undealt cards become the draw pile. Four shared building positions are available in the center, and each player receives four personal discard positions.

All building and discard positions begin empty. Players create them by playing or discarding cards during their turns.

PlayAural randomly selects the opening player for the first game. In a scored match, the opening position moves forward one seat after each game so the same seat does not always start.

\*\*Your turn\*\*

At the beginning of your turn, the game draws until you have five cards in hand. Cards left from an earlier turn stay in your hand, so you draw only the number needed to return to five. On your first turn, this normally means drawing all five cards.

You may then make as many legal building plays as you wish. A card may come from:

\* your hand;

\* the face-up card of your stock pile;

\* the top of any one of your four discard piles; or

\* in partnership play, the face-up stock card or top discard cards belonging to your partner.

Playing a card to a building pile does not end your turn. If you play every card from your hand during this part of the turn, you immediately draw back up to five and may continue. This can happen more than once in the same turn.

You are not required to make every available building play. When you are ready to stop, choose one card from your hand and one of your four discard piles. That discard ends your turn. Discarding the last card in your hand does not trigger an immediate refill; you draw at the beginning of your next turn instead.

In the rare case that your hand is empty and no card can be drawn, \*\*End turn without discarding\* becomes available.

\*\*Building piles and wild cards\*\*

An empty building position needs a 1. After that, the pile accepts the next number only: 2 after 1, 3 after 2, and so on through 12. If a pile shows 6, for example, it accepts a 7 or a Skip-Bo wild used as 7.

A wild takes the exact value needed by the destination pile. Each destination choice names that value, such as \*\*play Skip-Bo as 7\*\*; you choose the pile, not a number separately.

When a 12 is played, that building pile is complete. Its cards are set aside, the building position becomes empty, and a new sequence may begin there with 1.

If the draw pile becomes empty, cards from completed building piles are shuffled to make a new draw pile. Active building piles are never disturbed. If no completed pile is waiting, no card can be drawn until a building pile is completed and its cards are reshuffled.

\*\*Stock and discard piles\*\*

The top stock card and the number of cards remaining in every stock pile are public. Cards beneath the top remain hidden. Whenever a stock card is played, the next one is revealed immediately. Playing the final required stock card ends the game at once, even if other cards could still be played.

Your four discard piles do not follow numerical order. Any hand card may be placed on any discard pile, including an empty one or one with the same number already on top. Only the top card is available to play, so covered cards remain inaccessible until the cards above them are removed.

A useful strategy is to build descending runs across your discard piles. For example, placing 8 above 9 can let you play the 8 and then expose the 9 when a building sequence reaches those numbers. Wild cards are especially flexible, so covering one without a plan can be costly.

\*\*Scoring and scored matches\*\*

The winner of each game receives 25 points for winning plus 5 points for every card left in all opposing stock piles. In a partnership game, cards remaining in every opposing partnership are counted.

Scores carry between games only in the \*\*Scored match\* format. Stock piles, hands, discard piles, and building piles are dealt fresh for the next game. The first player or partnership to reach or pass the match target wins. The official target is 500 points.

\*\*Game options\*\*

\* \*\*Stock piles:\*\* \*\*Standard\* deals 30 cards with 2 to 4 players and 20 with 5 or 6. \*\*Quick 10\* and \*\*Quick 15\* use exactly that many cards for every player. Default: Standard.

\* \*\*Match format:\*\* \*\*Single game\* ends after the first stock-pile victory. \*\*Scored match\* carries scores across games until the target is reached. Default: Single game.

\* \*\*Match target:\*\* Points needed to win a scored match, from 25 to 5,000. This option appears only when Scored match is selected. Default: 500.

\* \*\*Team mode:\*\* \*\*Individual\* gives every player a separate result. Four players may choose two teams of two, and six players may choose three teams of two. Default: Individual.

The game will not start with an unsupported player count, partnership layout, stock setting, scoring format, or match target.

\*\*Turn menu and accessibility\*\*

The turn menu gives each available card exactly one row. It includes every card in your hand and any face-up stock or top discard card that can be played. A row names the card and its source without repeating the same card for several possible destinations.

Select a card with several legal destinations to choose where it goes. Building choices report the pile's current top and the card value to be played there. A hand card also offers your four discard piles; choosing one of them discards the card and ends your turn. If a stock or discard card has only one legal destination, selecting it plays the card immediately.

Your hand remains visible when another player has the turn. Those card rows keep the same stable identities they use on your own turn, helping screen-reader focus remain predictable. Selecting one out of turn simply tells you that it is not your turn; it never opens a destination selector or changes the game.

After a successful building play, focus first follows another legal play to the same building pile when one exists. Otherwise it moves to the next useful legal play or hand-card action. Pile status windows also keep one stable row for each pile while their values change.

Desktop clients keep pile-inspection commands in the actions menu and on keyboard shortcuts, leaving the turn menu focused on cards. Touch clients also place those commands in the turn menu, followed by Check scores when relevant, Whose turn, and Who's at the table.

\*\*Information actions\*\*

\* \*\*View building piles:\*\* Shows each building pile's current top and next required number, the draw-pile count, and how many completed cards are waiting to be shuffled.

\* \*\*View stock piles:\*\* Shows every player's public face-up stock card and total remaining stock count.

\* \*\*View your discard piles:\*\* Shows the top card and size of each of your four discard piles.

\* \*\*View another player's discard piles:\*\* Lets you choose a player and inspect that player's four public discard piles. With only one available player, the view opens directly. Spectators may choose any active player.

\* \*\*Check scores:\*\* Available in a scored match. The brief and detailed score views show progress toward the match target.

\* \*\*Whose turn:\*\* Reports the current player.

\* \*\*Who's at the table:\*\* Lists the players and spectators currently at the table.

\*\*Keyboard shortcuts\*\*

\* \*\*C:\*\* View the building piles.

\* \*\*E:\*\* View the stock piles.

\* \*\*V:\*\* View your discard piles.

\* \*\*Shift+V:\*\* View another player's discard piles. Spectators use the same shortcut to choose any active player.

\* \*\*S:\*\* Check scores during a scored match.

\* \*\*Shift+S:\*\* Open detailed scores during a scored match.

\* \*\*T:\*\* Check whose turn it is.

\* \*\*Ctrl+U:\*\* Hear who's at the table.

\* \*\*Escape:\*\* Open the actions menu.

\* \*\*Ctrl+F1:\*\* Open this guide.
