\*\*Truco\*\*

\*\*Variants\*\*

Truco is played in three variants, chosen with the Variant option before the game starts. Argentine Truco is the default. Brazilian Truco (Paulista) and Spanish Truco (Valencian Truc) follow below. Each section explains the rules of that variant and its own options. The Variant option is the first game option, and the other options change to match the variant you select. Touch gestures and descriptions work the same way in all three variants.

\*\*Argentine Truco\*\*

\*\*What is Argentine Truco?\*\*

Truco is a trick-taking card game with bluffing, played with the 40-card Spanish deck. Two sides compete to reach the game target first. Each hand is a small contest of its own: you play up to three tricks, and you may also challenge the other side with calls such as Truco and Envido to raise the stakes. Truco is a game of nerve as much as of good cards.

PlayAural supports 2, 4, or 6 players. With two players it is a one-on-one game. With four or six players you play in two teams, seated alternately.

\*\*How to win\*\*

Score points over many hands. The first side to reach the game target wins. You choose a target of 15 or 30 points in the game options. Points come from winning hands, and also from winning envido and flor.

\*\*Glossary\*\*

\* \*\*Hand:\*\* One deal of three cards to every player, played until a side wins it.

\* \*\*Trick:\*\* One round in which every player plays a single card. Two tricks win a hand.

\* \*\*Mano:\*\* The player who plays first in the hand, the one after the dealer. The deal moves forward after every hand.

\* \*\*Parda:\*\* A tied trick.

\* \*\*Truco, Retruco, Vale cuatro:\*\* The three calls that raise the value of the hand.

\* \*\*Quiero:\*\* "I accept." \*\*No quiero:\*\* "I decline."

\* \*\*Envido:\*\* A side bet on whose first three cards add up to the most points.

\* \*\*Flor:\*\* Three cards of the same suit. An optional rule.

\* \*\*Me voy al mazo:\*\* Folding your own cards for the hand.

\* \*\*Malas and buenas:\*\* In a 30-point game, points 1 to 15 are the malas and points 16 to 30 are the buenas.

\*\*Cards and their strength\*\*

The deck has 40 cards: the 1 to 7 and the 10, 11, and 12 of each suit (Coins, Cups, Swords, and Clubs). The 10, 11, and 12 are the figures. The 8s and 9s are not used. There is no wild card and no trump card, and all cards are played face up.

From strongest to weakest, cards rank like this:

1. The 1 of Swords.

2. The 1 of Clubs.

3. The 7 of Swords.

4. The 7 of Coins.

5. All the 3s.

6. All the 2s.

7. The 1 of Cups and the 1 of Coins.

8. All the 12s.

9. All the 11s.

10. All the 10s.

11. The 7 of Cups and the 7 of Clubs.

12. All the 6s.

13. All the 5s.

14. All the 4s.

Cards in the same group tie.

\*\*Playing a hand\*\*

Each player receives three cards. The mano plays first. Players then take turns around the table, and the highest card wins the trick. The winner of a trick plays first in the next one. If a trick is tied, the player who played first in that trick plays first again.

You win a hand by winning two tricks. If the first trick is tied, whoever wins the next trick wins the hand. If you win the first trick and the second is tied, you win the hand. If all three tricks are tied, the side of the mano wins. Cards a player has already put on the table stay in the trick even if that player later folds.

The hand is normally worth 1 point. Truco calls make it worth more.

\*\*Truco, Retruco, and Vale cuatro\*\*

Any player who is still in the hand may call Truco at any time, even when it is not their turn, as long as no other call is waiting for an answer. The other side answers:

\* \*\*Quiero:\*\* The hand is now worth 2 points.

\* \*\*No quiero:\*\* The side that called wins 1 point and the hand ends.

\* \*\*Raise:\*\* Instead of answering, the other side may call Retruco. This also accepts the Truco.

The same pattern continues up the ladder: Truco is worth 2 points, Retruco 3, and Vale cuatro 4. Refusing a call gives the other side the value of the previous step: 1 point for refusing Truco, 2 for refusing Retruco, and 3 for refusing Vale cuatro.

Only the side that accepted the last call may raise it. For example, after your side accepts a Truco, only your side may call Retruco. Vale cuatro is the highest call.

\*\*Envido\*\*

Envido is a bet on who holds the best two-card total. It can only be called in the first trick of the hand, and only by a player who has not yet played a card. A truco that has not been accepted yet may be interrupted by an envido: the envido is settled first, and the Truco still waits for its answer afterwards. Once a Truco has been accepted, envido can no longer be called.

The calls are:

\* \*\*Envido:\*\* Worth 2 points. It can be called twice.

\* \*\*Real envido:\*\* Worth 3 points. Calls add together, so Envido plus Real envido is worth 5.

\* \*\*Falta envido:\*\* Worth what the leading side still needs to reach the game target. It cannot be raised further.

The other side answers with Quiero, No quiero, or a raise. If your first call is refused, you score 1 point. If a raise is refused, the side that raised scores the points already accepted before it, with a minimum of 1.

In a 30-point game, when both sides are still in the malas, an accepted Falta envido decides the whole game: the winner receives every point needed to reach 30.

\*\*Counting the envido.\*\* The game counts everything for you. The numbered cards 1 to 7 are worth their number, and the figures 10, 11, and 12 are worth zero. If you have two cards of the same suit, you score 20 plus their two values. With three cards of one suit, the two highest count. With no two cards of the same suit, only your highest card counts. The most possible is 33. Calling with low points is allowed, so bluffing is part of the game. If sides tie, the player who comes first in order from the mano wins.

The winning cards are announced at the end of the hand.

\*\*Flor (optional)\*\*

When Flor is turned on, a player dealt three cards of the same suit has a flor. Flores are announced automatically as soon as the cards are dealt, and they cancel the envido for that hand.

\* If only one side has a flor, it scores 3 points for each flor at once.

\* If both sides have a flor, the Truco cannot be called until the first card is played. Until then, a player with a flor may call \*\*Contraflor\*\*. The other side's flor holders answer with Quiero, No quiero, or a raise to \*\*Contraflor al resto\*\*. The side with the better flor wins 6 points for a Contraflor, or the points the leading side still needs for Contraflor al resto. A refused Contraflor gives the caller 4 points. A refused Contraflor al resto gives 4 points when it was called directly, and 6 points when it raised a Contraflor.

\* If nobody calls Contraflor, each side scores 3 points for each of its flores when the first card is played.

Only players with a flor can answer or raise a flor. A flor counts 20 plus the values of all three cards, with figures worth zero. For example, 7, 6, and 5 of one suit make 38. The higher flor wins.

\*\*Me voy al mazo\*\*

You may fold your own cards at any time when no call is waiting for an answer. Only your cards leave the hand. Your teammates keep playing, and you are skipped. Your envido does not count once you fold.

If every member of one side folds, the other side wins the hand. It receives 1 point for the envido if envido was still open, plus the value of the hand: 1 point, or the level of the Truco if one was accepted.

\*\*Scoring and game length\*\*

When the hand ends, the side that won it scores its value. A game ends as soon as one side reaches the target, even in the middle of a hand, and points never go beyond the target.

\*\*Game options\*\*

\* \*\*Game length:\*\* 15 or 30 points. A 30-point game has malas and buenas. Default: 15.

\* \*\*Flor:\*\* Turns the flor rules on or off. Default: off.

\* \*\*Team mode:\*\* Individual for 2 players, 2v2 for 4 players, or 3v3 for 6 players. The game will not start unless the team mode matches the number of players. The host may review and rearrange the teams before starting. Default: Individual.

\*\*Turn menu and accessibility\*\*

On your turn, the turn menu lists the cards in your hand. Select one to play it. The calls you can make right now appear after your cards: Envido, Real envido, Falta envido, Contraflor, Contraflor al resto, and Truco, Retruco, or Vale cuatro. Only calls that are allowed at that moment are shown, so the list changes during the hand. Me voy al mazo comes last.

When the other side calls and waits for your answer, the menu changes. Quiero and No quiero appear first, followed by the raises you may make. Your cards are hidden until the call is answered. If your teammate answers first, the call is settled for the whole team.

Any player on a side may call or answer for the side, whether or not it is their turn. If a human player is on the side that must answer, bots on that side wait for the human. On a touch device, the information actions also appear in the turn menu.

On touch devices, press and hold any item in the turn menu (on mobile with self-voicing, double-tap and hold) to hear what it is without using it. For a card, you hear how strong it is and its envido value. For a call, you hear what it is worth and what refusing it costs. For the answers and for folding, you hear what they do. Three-finger tap on mobile self-voicing, or F1 on desktop, reads the same description.

The game announces the cards you are dealt, the calls and answers, the points scored, and the scores at the end of every hand.

\*\*Information actions\*\*

\* \*\*Read your cards:\*\* Reads the cards in your hand.

\* \*\*Read the cards on the table:\*\* Reads the cards played in each trick.

\* \*\*Read current calls:\*\* Reports the value of the hand, any call waiting for an answer, and whether envido is still open.

\* \*\*Check scores:\*\* Reports the score of every side.

\* \*\*Whose turn:\*\* Reports whose turn it is to play.

\* \*\*Who's at the table:\*\* Lists the players and spectators currently at the table.

\*\*Brazilian Truco (Paulista)\*\*

\*\*What is Paulista Truco?\*\*

Truco Paulista is the Brazilian version of Truco, a trick-taking card game full of bluffs. It is played with a 40-card French deck. Two sides race to 12 points. In each hand you play up to three tricks, and you can challenge the other side with Truco to raise what the hand is worth. It is as much a game of nerve as of good cards.

PlayAural supports 2, 4 or 6 players. With two players it is one against one. With four or six players you play in two teams, seated alternately.

\*\*How you win\*\*

Score points over several hands. The first side to reach 12 points wins the game. Points come from winning hands. Winning a hand is normally worth 1 point, and Truco calls make it worth more.

\*\*Glossary\*\*

\* \*\*Hand:\*\* One deal of three cards to each player, played until one side wins it.

\* \*\*Trick:\*\* One round in which every player plays a single card. Two tricks win the hand.

\* \*\*Vira:\*\* A card turned face up at the start of each hand. It is not dealt to anyone.

\* \*\*Manilha:\*\* The strongest cards of the hand. They are the cards whose rank comes right after the vira's rank.

\* \*\*Truco, Seis, Nove, Doze:\*\* The four calls that raise the value of the hand to 3, 6, 9 and 12 points.

\* \*\*Correr:\*\* "Run": refuse a call or give up the hand.

\* \*\*Mão de onze:\*\* A hand in which one side has 11 points.

\* \*\*Mão de ferro:\*\* A hand in which both sides have 11 points. It is played with the cards face down.

\*\*The cards and their strength\*\*

The deck has 40 cards: the ace, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, jack, queen and king of each suit (diamonds, spades, hearts and clubs). The 8, 9 and 10 are not used. There are no jokers and no trump suit, and every card is played face up.

From strongest to weakest, ordinary cards rank like this: 3, 2, ace, king, jack, queen, 7, 6, 5, 4. Cards of the same rank tie, unless they are manilhas.

Each hand, the vira decides the manilhas. The manilha is the rank that follows the vira in this order: 4, 5, 6, 7, queen, jack, king, ace, 2, 3, and then back to 4. For example, if the vira is a 7, the manilhas are the queens. If the vira is a 3, the manilhas are the 4s.

The manilhas beat every other card. When there are four manilhas, the suit decides, from strongest to weakest: clubs, hearts, spades and diamonds. Manilhas never tie with each other.

\*\*How a hand is played\*\*

Each player gets three cards and a vira is turned up. The player after the dealer, called the mano, plays first. Then players take turns around the table and the strongest card wins the trick. Whoever wins a trick plays first in the next one. If a trick is tied, the player who played first in that trick plays first again.

You win a hand by winning two tricks. If the first trick is tied, whoever wins the next trick wins the hand. If you win the first trick and the second is tied, you win the hand. If all three tricks are tied, the hand is annulled: nobody scores and a new hand is dealt.

\*\*Truco, Seis, Nove and Doze\*\*

Any player still in the hand may call Truco at any moment, even when it is not their turn, as long as no other call is waiting for an answer. The other side answers:

\* \*\*Accept:\*\* The hand is now worth 3 points.

\* \*\*Run:\*\* The side that called wins 1 point and the hand ends.

\* \*\*Raise:\*\* Instead of answering, the other side may call Seis. This also accepts the Truco.

The same pattern follows up the ladder: Truco is worth 3 points, Seis 6, Nove 9 and Doze 12. Refusing a call gives the other side the value of the step below it: 1 point for refusing Truco, 3 for refusing Seis, 6 for refusing Nove and 9 for refusing Doze.

Only the side that accepted the last call may raise it. For example, after your side accepts a Truco, only your side can call Seis. Doze is the highest call.

\*\*Mão de onze\*\*

When a side reaches 11 points and the other side has fewer, the hand is a mão de onze. The side with 11 points must decide before any card is played. Any live player on that side may answer for the whole side:

\* \*\*Play (Jogar):\*\* The hand is played for 3 points. Nobody may call Truco during this hand.

\* \*\*Run (Correr):\*\* The other side wins 1 point and the hand ends.

Before answering, the players on the side with 11 points may look at each other's cards. When the hand is read, you hear your partners' cards as well as your own. The other side never sees them.

If both sides have 11 points, the hand is a mão de ferro. All cards are dealt face down and nobody may look at them: the game lists your cards as "face-down card 1", "face-down card 2" and so on, and each card is revealed to everyone only when it is played. No calls are allowed, and whoever wins the hand wins the game.

\*\*Scoring and game length\*\*

When a hand ends, the side that won it scores its value. The game ends as soon as a side reaches 12 points, and points never go above 12.

\*\*Game options\*\*

\* \*\*Team mode:\*\* Individual for 2 players, 2v2 for 4 players or 3v3 for 6 players. The game will not start if the team mode does not match the number of players. The host can review and rearrange the teams before starting. Default: Individual.

\*\*Turn menu and accessibility\*\*

On your turn, the turn menu shows the cards in your hand. Select one to play it. The call you can make right now appears after your cards: Truco, Seis, Nove or Doze. Only calls that are allowed at that moment are shown, so the list changes during the hand.

When the other side calls and waits for your answer, the menu changes. Accept and Run appear first, followed by the raise you may make. Your cards are hidden until the call is answered. If a teammate answers first, the call is settled for the whole team. In a mão de onze, the choices are Jogar and Correr.

Any player on a side may call or answer for their side, whether or not it is their turn. If there is a human on the side that has to answer, the bots on that side wait for the human. On touch devices, the information actions also appear in the turn menu.

On touch devices, press and hold any item in the turn menu (on mobile with self-voicing, double-tap and hold) to hear what it is without using it. For a card, you hear how strong it is. For a call, you hear what it is worth and what refusing it costs. For the answers and for folding, you hear what they do. Three-finger tap on mobile self-voicing, or F1 on desktop, reads the same description.

The game announces the vira and the manilha, your cards, calls and answers, points scored and the score at the end of every hand.

\*\*Information actions\*\*

\* \*\*Read your cards:\*\* Reads the cards in your hand.

\* \*\*Read cards on the table:\*\* Reads the cards played in each trick.

\* \*\*Read current calls:\*\* Reports the value of the hand and any call waiting for an answer.

\* \*\*Read the vira and manilha:\*\* Reads the turned-up card and which rank is the manilha.

\* \*\*Check scores:\*\* Reports the score of each side.

\* \*\*Whose turn:\*\* Reports who has to play.

\* \*\*Who is at the table:\*\* Lists the players and spectators at the table.

\* \*\*V:\*\* Read the vira and manilha.

\*\*Spanish Truco (Valencian Truc)\*\*

\*\*What is Valencian Truc?\*\*

Truc is the traditional card game of the Valencian Country, a cousin of Truco. It is a trick-taking game full of bluffs, played with a small 22-card Spanish deck. Two sides compete to reach the game total. In each hand you play up to three tricks, and you can challenge the other side with Truc and Envit to raise the stakes. It is as much a game of nerve as of good cards.

PlayAural supports 2 or 4 players. With two players it is one against one. With four players you play in two teams of two, seated alternately.

\*\*How you win\*\*

Score points over several hands. The game is won by winning two cames. A came is played to a number of points that you choose in the options: 24 or 18. When a side reaches it, that side wins the came and both scores start again from zero. Points come from winning hands and from winning the envit.

\*\*Glossary\*\*

\* \*\*Hand:\*\* One deal of three cards to each player, played until one side wins it.

\* \*\*Trick:\*\* One round in which every player plays a single card. Two tricks win the hand.

\* \*\*Mano:\*\* The player who plays first in the hand, the one after the dealer. The deal moves on after every hand.

\* \*\*Truc, Retruc, Quatre val, Joc fora:\*\* The four calls that raise the value of the hand.

\* \*\*Vull:\*\* "I accept". \*\*No vull:\*\* "I refuse".

\* \*\*Envit:\*\* A side bet on who has the better total with the first cards dealt. \*\*Torna:\*\* A raise of the envit. \*\*La falta:\*\* The biggest envit call.

\* \*\*Me'n vaig:\*\* "I'm leaving": give up the hand.

\* \*\*Came:\*\* One part of the game, played to 24 or 18 points as chosen in the options. Win two cames to win the game.

\*\*The cards and their strength\*\*

The deck has 22 cards: the 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 of each suit (coins, cups, swords and clubs), plus the ace of swords and the ace of clubs. There are no jokers and no trump suit, and every card is played face up.

From strongest to weakest, the cards rank like this:

1. The ace of swords.

2. The ace of clubs.

3. The 7 of swords.

4. The 7 of coins.

5. All the 3s.

6. The 7 of cups and the 7 of clubs.

7. All the 6s.

8. All the 5s.

9. All the 4s.

Cards in the same group tie.

\*\*How a hand is played\*\*

Each player gets three cards. The mano plays first. Then players take turns around the table and the strongest card wins the trick. Whoever wins a trick plays first in the next one. If a trick is tied, the player who played first in that trick plays first again.

You win a hand by winning two tricks. If the first trick is tied, whoever wins the next trick wins the hand. If you win the first trick and the second is tied, you win the hand. If all three tricks are tied, the side of the player who comes first from the mano among those who tied the last trick wins.

A hand is normally worth 1 point. The truc calls make it worth more.

\*\*Truc, Retruc, Quatre val and Joc fora\*\*

Any player still in the hand may call Truc at any moment, even when it is not their turn, as long as no other call is waiting for an answer. The other side answers:

\* \*\*Vull:\*\* The hand is now worth 2 points.

\* \*\*No vull:\*\* The side that called wins 1 point and the hand ends.

\* \*\*Raise:\*\* Instead of answering, the other side may call Retruc. This also accepts the Truc.

The same pattern follows up the ladder: Truc is worth 2 points, Retruc 3, Quatre val 4, and Joc fora is worth the whole game. Refusing a call gives the other side the value of the step below it: 1 point for refusing Truc, 2 for refusing Retruc, 3 for refusing Quatre val and 4 for refusing Joc fora.

Only the side that accepted the last call may raise it. Joc fora is the highest call. If it is accepted, the side that wins the hand wins the whole game.

\*\*Envit\*\*

The envit is a side bet on who has the best total of two cards. It can only be called in the first trick of the hand, and only by a player who has not yet played a card. A Truc that has not been accepted yet can be interrupted by an envit: the envit is settled first and the Truc waits for its answer afterwards. Once a Truc has been accepted, the envit can no longer be called.

The calls are:

\* \*\*Envit:\*\* Worth 2 points.

\* \*\*Torna:\*\* The other side raises the envit. It replaces the envit and is worth 4 points in total.

\* \*\*La falta:\*\* Worth what the leading side still needs to finish the current came. It cannot be raised.

The other side answers with Vull, No vull or a raise. If your envit is refused, you score 1 point. If a torna or la falta is refused, the side that made it scores what the call before it was worth: 2 points after an envit and 4 points after a torna. If la falta is the first call and it is refused, it scores 1 point.

A note on the rules: the values of the Envit, Torna and La falta, and the points scored when they are refused, follow the Truc regulation published on fallas.com and the Salvagim guide of the Universitat de València. The tournament rulebooks of Torrent and of the Universitat Politècnica de València play without envit. Joc fora wins the whole game, as in the UPV and fallas.com rulebooks.

\*\*How the envit is counted.\*\* The game counts for you. Cards from 3 to 7 are worth their number and the aces are worth 1. If you have two cards of the same suit, you add 20 plus their two values. If you have no two cards of the same suit, only your highest card counts. You can call with a poor envit, so bluffing is part of the game. In a tie, whoever comes first in the order from the mano wins.

The winning cards are announced at the end of the hand.

\*\*Me'n vaig\*\*

You can give up the hand at any time when no call is waiting for an answer. When you do, your whole team gives up the hand. The other side wins the hand. It also scores 1 point for the envit if it was still open, plus the value of the hand: 1 point, or the level of the Truc if one had been accepted.

\*\*Scoring and game length\*\*

When a hand ends, the side that won it scores its value. The game ends as soon as a side reaches the game total, even in the middle of a hand, and points never go above the total.

\*\*Game options\*\*

\* \*\*Came length:\*\* 24 or 18 points. Default: 24.

\* \*\*Team mode:\*\* Individual for 2 players or 2v2 for 4 players. The game will not start if the team mode does not match the number of players. The host can review and rearrange the teams before starting. Default: Individual.
